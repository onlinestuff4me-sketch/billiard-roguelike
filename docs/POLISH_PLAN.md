# Polish plan

A review of the level designs, sound, animation, UI and feel of Lightline, and a plan to bring them up a level without changing its style. Generated by `tools/review/build.mjs` from `tools/review/items.mjs` and the measured tables in `tools/review/levels.json`; edit those, not this file.

**Review page** (every item side by side, current vs proposed, with Approve / Maybe / Reject): https://claude.ai/artifact/15qPVfkdv8MmpqZWjjbvmW (private to the owner). Decisions are recorded in the last section as they arrive.

**Rules this plan keeps:** aiming and shot physics do not change (the preview must agree 100% with the played shot); every prototype is behind a switch in `src/ui/Polish.js` and off by default; nothing here undoes a review decision in `docs/DESIGN_BIBLE.md` without saying so (two items touch one: A4 and A5).

## 1. How the game was reviewed

- **Played in the real build.** A headless Chromium at a phone viewport (390×780) played the built game: the home and play screens, a run from Table 1, shots (a straight pot, a two-rail bank at full power), the table result, the shop, the Boot transition into Table 2, a scratch, and all 13 tables through the tuning tool. Frames were captured on a virtual clock (`tools/capture/lib.mjs`), so what was reviewed frame by frame is what the game draws, at the speed it draws it.
- **Read against the bible.** `docs/DESIGN_BIBLE.md` is the standard: its laws, its moment catalog, its record of past review picks, and its own gap table (§11), which already names two of the items below (the rail light-run and the balls flickering up at Boot).
- **Levels were measured, not eyeballed.** Every table as authored, and every proposed layout, went through `tools/tune-tables.mjs` (beam search in the real physics) for its robust line, its ceiling and its widest openers.
- **Sound builds on the sound pass.** `docs/SOUND.md` (branch `sound-pass`) audited all 37 sounds and its review page has no picks yet. Its proposals are folded in here by family rather than redone, and one item is added: sound for the new animation beats, in step with them.

**What is already good and is left alone.** The count (plates, the slam, the tally), the result ledger and its skip, the shop's money travelling to and from the wallet, the Boot transition, the scratch glitch, the freeze frame, the table cards, and the cue stick. These are finished, specific and consistent; the plan does not touch them beyond their sounds.

**What the review found, in short.**

1. *The table under-reacts to the things that happen on it most.* Rails, contacts and pockets (the three events of every shot) get a handful of particles each, while rarer moments (the slam, CLEAR!, cut-ins) are lavish. Items A1, A2, F1.
2. *Things appear without arriving.* The rack on a new table, the home screen, and every menu panel pop in on one frame, against the bible's own law 8. Items A3, U1.
3. *Almost nothing moves at rest* (only the cue ball's glow). Item A4.
4. *One win is unmarked.* Passing the target mid-table. Item U2.
5. *The full-power strike whites out the screen.* Item A5.
6. *The run repeats itself.* Several tables share a rack or a silhouette, and targets sit far below what a careful player scores, so "beat the target" is rarely a decision. Level items.
7. *Sound has the hierarchy upside down* (the sound pass's finding): the pot is one of the quietest sounds, a hard rail one of the loudest, most weight is below what a phone can play, and old prototype voices stand in for a dozen events. Items S1 to S8.

### L0 · Targets that ask for something

- **Problem.** Targets sit far below what a careful player scores, so beating the target is rarely a decision. Measured against each table's robust line (every shot with at least 3° to spare), today's targets run from 29% to 67% with no trend: Table 1 asks for 200 where the robust line makes 700, and one banked 8 alone pays 425. On Table 13 a single unused shot pays 650 against a target of 625. On 7 of the 13 tables one good pot or one spare shot already clears the bar.
- **Proposal.** Set each target from its table's measured robust total: 40% of it on Table 1, rising 3.5 points a table to 82% on Table 13, rounded to 25. Early tables stay forgiving; by the end the target takes most of a clean line. Re-measure and re-apply whenever a layout, shot count or scoring rule changes.
- **Why it fits.** The schema already says each target is set from the robust line with the margin shrinking as the run goes on, and PUZZLE_RAMP §4 asks for a "greed gap": the obvious pot drops and is not enough. The target only pays (points and cash); hearts are lost for balls left standing, so a higher target never ends a run on its own.
- **Effort.** small (under a day)
- **Risk.** Med. Two things. Cash comes from beating the target, so higher targets tighten the shop early (a plain one-pot-a-shot clear of Table 1 pays about 225, under the new 275; that costs cash, never a heart). And the curve is not smooth yet: it follows each table's robust total, which swings (Table 3 drops to 250, Tables 10 and 12 jump), so I would apply the rule after the layouts below are settled and re-measured, not before.
- **Where.** src/data/tables.json (target); tools/tune-tables.mjs

## 3. Sound

The sound pass already audited all 37 sounds and proposed replacements. They are folded in here by family, playable, with one new item: sound for the new animation beats.

### S1 · The mix: two buses, a limiter, thumps a phone can play

- **Problem.** Everything goes through one slow-motion low-pass, so the strike, the score and the UI are muffled whenever the table is. 50 to 75% of the big hits sit below 150 Hz, which a phone speaker cannot play. There is no limiter and no voice cap: a six-ball break peaks at −2 dBFS.
- **Proposal.** Two buses (the table goes through the slow-mo filter; strike, score and UI stay dry), a duck for the slam, CLEAR! and the last ball, a −4 dB limiter, caps of 3 to 4 clicks per 50 ms, a harmonic layer under every thump, and small random detune on repeats. Android also gets a short buzz on the strike, pots, slam and blasts.
- **Why it fits.** Design bible law 6: every moment earns a reaction you can see, hear and feel. Nothing about the sound palette changes; this is the wiring under it.
- **Effort.** medium (1 to 2 days)
- **Risk.** Low. Touches every voice at once. Mitigated by keeping every existing method name and the non-finite guard, and by checking levels with the same offline render the audit used.
- **Where.** src/core/AudioManager.js:52-66; SOUND.md §6 "The bus"
- **Sounds.** A six-ball break; The same break, heard as a moment. Playable on the review page; A/B variants on the sound pass page.

### S2 · The shot: strike, contact, rail, pocket

- **Problem.** The strike plays while the table is still filtered: 0% of its energy is above 1 kHz and it is 12 dB under the slam. A cue-ball rail is nearly inaudible (−41 to −47 dB), an object ball off a soft rail is silent, and the pot (−31 dB, an old "enemy death" voice) is 12 dB quieter than a hard rail.
- **Proposal.** A dry crack for the strike that grows with power. A sharp click with a short ring for ball on ball. A cushion "thup" for every ball on every rail. A drop, a rattle in the pocket and a note that climbs one step per pot in the stroke.
- **Why it fits.** Real ball contact is a sub-millisecond click, not a thud. The climbing note uses the minor pentatonic ladder the score already plays (bible §9).
- **Effort.** medium (1 to 2 days)
- **Risk.** Low. These are the most repeated sounds, so a wrong level is tiring. Voice caps and the detune are there for that.
- **Where.** main.js playerLaunch, impact, carom, playerRebound, wallSplat, removeBall
- **Sounds.** A full shot, strike to coins; strike; contact; rail; Pocket drop, three pots in a stroke. Playable on the review page; A/B variants on the sound pass page.

### S3 · Scoring: numbers, lock, slam, tally, coins

- **Problem.** MULT lands 5 dB quieter than POINTS though it is the bigger number. The slam is 59% sub-bass, so on a phone only its crack is left. The result tally plays 340 ms notes 70 ms apart, which smears into a wash. Coins are 20 dB under the slam and step in quarter-tones, off the scale.
- **Proposal.** A tick on every landing with a 2 ms attack, MULT at the same level with its own thump. A beat of quiet, then the slam as crack, harmonic sub and chord. Short tally ticks with a ding on the last. A classic two-note coin, a semitone up per coin.
- **Why it fits.** Same pitches and the same ladder as today (bible §7: "a note one scale degree higher plays"). The quiet before the slam is the audio half of the 90 ms hit-stop the count already takes.
- **Effort.** small (under a day)
- **Risk.** Low. Low: each voice is a drop-in for an existing call.
- **Where.** ScoreFeed.js:565-606; Moments.js:1350, 1541, 1584
- **Sounds.** Three numbers into POINTS; Three numbers into MULT; lock; slam; tally; coins. Playable on the review page; A/B variants on the sound pass page.

### S4 · Mistakes get their own sounds

- **Problem.** A scratch plays five voices over two seconds, including the score slam, a reward sound. Losing a table plays the old death dive, the CLEAR fanfare and a door at once: the loudest moment in the game. A foul, a scratch, a mine and a kickback share one voice. A heart breaking is silent.
- **Proposal.** Scratch: a drop and a deny, then a reversed swell as the ball comes back. Foul: two low buzzes, then the ball pops back. A lost table: one crack and a falling line. The heart: a glass crack and a thud. Run over: a slow falling line.
- **Why it fits.** Bible §8.2 says a scratch must say "your ball went in, this shot pays nothing, here is your ball back": a drop, a deny and a return are those three things.
- **Effort.** small (under a day)
- **Risk.** Low. Low. Removes three stacked calls on the fail path.
- **Where.** main.js:2084, 2181, 2860; Moments.js:417, 1154, 1402, 1621-1681
- **Sounds.** A scratch, start to respawn; foul; Losing a table; heart; runover. Playable on the review page; A/B variants on the sound pass page.

### S5 · Big wins: last ball, CLEAR!, combos, run complete

- **Problem.** The last ball of a table sounds like any other pot. CLEAR! is a short three-note figure doubled with a door sound, and the word itself slams in silently. A combo has no sound of its own. Finishing the whole run sounds the same as finishing one table.
- **Proposal.** Last ball: the table hushes for a beat, then a big drop with a high chord. CLEAR!: a run up into a chord. A combo turns the pot note into a chord stab. Run complete gets a three-chord fanfare.
- **Why it fits.** The picture already does this: the table runs at 8x after the last ball and CLEAR! slams in (bible §8.5c). The sound follows the same hold-then-hit shape.
- **Effort.** small (under a day)
- **Risk.** Low. The last-ball hush ducks the mix for 120 ms; it must not swallow the pot itself. Sequenced so the drop lands after the dip.
- **Where.** main.js:2123; Juice.js:746; Moments.js:417, 674
- **Sounds.** Clearing a table, last ball to cash; last; clear; combo; runwin. Playable on the review page; A/B variants on the sound pass page.

### S6 · Things on the table

- **Problem.** A mine, a kicker and a hard rail all play the same sub thud. The ×2 ring plays a leftover saw that has nothing to do with the MULT box. The bomb shot, an ability you buy, plays the old game-over sound. The freeze pickup shares a voice with five other events. Portals and ability cut-ins are silent.
- **Proposal.** One voice each: a mult chime for ×2 (the MULT box's own timbre), a blast for the mine, a spring for the kicker, an ice ting, a swell into the freeze hit, a phase-through for portals, a pinball pop for bumpers, an explosion for the bomb, a swish and stamp for cut-ins.
- **Why it fits.** Bible law 1: color says which number a thing feeds. Sound follows the same rule: a thing that feeds Mult sounds like the MULT box, a hazard sounds like a hazard.
- **Effort.** medium (1 to 2 days)
- **Risk.** Low. The portal needs a new physics event (a notification only; the crossing itself is untouched, and check-portal stays the gate).
- **Where.** main.js:2153, 2214-2256, 2283; Moments.js:1726, 1790; Juice.js:689; PhysicsSystem (portal has no event)
- **Sounds.** x2; mine; kicker; ice; freeze; portal; bumper; plus; bomb; cutin. Playable on the review page; A/B variants on the sound pass page.

### S7 · Menus, cards and the shop

- **Problem.** Menu taps are silent. The table card borrows the strike sound. Buy, achievement and hold share one voice; reroll, sell, the shop opening and the card share another. The aim-exit sound fires on every release and blurs the strike.
- **Proposal.** A 30 ms tick on every button, a lower one for Back. A whoosh and stamp for the table card. A ka-ching for a buy, a slowing wheel for a reroll, coins back for an undo, two bells for an achievement. Aim: a soft air swell in, a small rise on cancel, nothing on release.
- **Why it fits.** Quiet and short by design: the bible keeps the HUD at rest brightness, and these stay under the table sounds.
- **Effort.** small (under a day)
- **Risk.** Low. Low.
- **Where.** main.js:2702-2831, 5153-5178; Moments.js:311, 336, 1865, 1906; index.html menus (silent)
- **Sounds.** tap; card; buy; reroll; sell; ach; tables; aim. Playable on the review page; A/B variants on the sound pass page.

### S8 · New: a sound on every new animation beat, on the frame it lands

- **Problem.** The sound pass was written before the animation proposals below, so their beats have no voice: balls rezzing in, the score passing the target, the home screen booting. Today several sounds also start early or late against the picture (the table card's sound leads the card by 0.4 s; the pot sound plays when the ball is removed, not when it reaches the mouth).
- **Proposal.** One rule: a sound starts on the frame its picture lands, and a hit-stop holds both. New voices: a climbing tick per ball as the rack rezzes in; a click and rising fifth when the score passes the target; nine ticks and a swell as the title boots; a bright zing on the rail when light runs along it (the sound pass's "thup + light zing"). Every animation card on this page plays with these in step: use its Hear it buttons.
- **Why it fits.** Bible §6: one movement at a time, each finishing before the next. Sound on the same frame is what makes two things read as one event.
- **Effort.** small (under a day)
- **Risk.** Low. Low. Each is one call at a point where the animation already runs.
- **Where.** src/ui/Polish.js (rezTick, targetBeaten, uiTick hooks); AudioManager.js
- **Sounds.** The rack rezzes in (5 balls); Score passes the target; Home screen boots; Rail, with the light-run zing. Playable on the review page; A/B variants on the sound pass page.

## 4. Animation and juice

What the table does when something happens on it. Each proposal is a working prototype in src/ui/Polish.js, off by default.

### A1 · Rails: light runs along the cushion

- **Problem.** A ball off a rail throws five small sparks and that is all. The design bible lists this as an open gap: "the rail flashes, then a mult light runs along it" is specified and not built. Rails are how you build Mult, and the table barely notices them.
- **Proposal.** The cushion flashes where it was hit and light runs along it both ways, farther the harder the hit. Orange for the cue ball, whose rails feed Mult; plain structure light for any other ball. The +1 WALL plate still appears only when a pot cashes it in.
- **Why it fits.** Closes a row in the bible's own gap table (§11). Orange is the Mult channel (law 1), and the effect lights its cause with no number, which is what §7 asks for before a pot.
- **Effort.** small (under a day)
- **Risk.** Low. Screen noise on a multi-rail shot. Each run lasts under 0.6 s and is skipped for slow touches.
- **Where.** main.js playerRebound, wallSplat, enemyRebound; Polish.rail
- **Prototype.** `window.__polish('rail', 'run')` (`lightline-polish-v1`), off by default.

### A2 · Pockets: the mouth gulps the ball

- **Problem.** The pot is the moment the whole game is about, and the pocket itself does little: the ball shrinks away and one thin ring goes out. The pocket beam from the juice review was turned off, so nothing replaced it.
- **Proposal.** A ring in the ball's own color closes on the mouth as the ball falls, the mouth flashes, a ring goes out and ticks of light chase round the lip. The last ball of a table gets a wider ring and more ticks. No pillar and no beam.
- **Why it fits.** Bible §8.6 specifies "pocket flare, shockwave, ball derez into the void". Circles are table things (law 2), and it is one event under 300 ms at glow-3 (law 3).
- **Effort.** small (under a day)
- **Risk.** Low. The beam was rejected in review; this stays flat on the felt, inside the pocket's own footprint, so it should not read as the same idea. Worth a look for that reason.
- **Where.** main.js potted handler, removeBall, sinkBall; Polish.pocket
- **Prototype.** `window.__polish('pocket', 'gulp')` (`lightline-polish-v1`), off by default.

### A3 · Table open: the balls rez in, by number

- **Problem.** When a table opens, its balls are simply there. The bible's Boot treatment says "balls flicker up" and lists it as not built. Every other arrival in the game (the stick, the cue ball after a scratch, cards, plates) is shown arriving.
- **Proposal.** Before the table card comes in, each ball drops in on a short beam, lowest number first, with a ring closing on its spot and a tick that climbs in pitch. About half a second for five balls, then the card.
- **Why it fits.** Bible law 8: nothing appears without being shown to appear. It is the scratch respawn's beam-and-ring (§8.2) at a smaller size, and it teaches the rack's order at a glance on the tables where order matters.
- **Effort.** small (under a day)
- **Risk.** Low. Adds about 0.5 s before the card on each table. The balls are on the table for the physics and the aim line from the first frame; only their drawing waits.
- **Where.** main.js showRoomBanner; Polish.rack
- **Prototype.** `window.__polish('rack', 'rez')` (`lightline-polish-v1`), off by default.

### A4 · Idle: the table stays alive between shots

- **Problem.** At rest the only thing that moves is a slow pulse in the cue ball's glow. Clearing the stick and the aim lines from the resting table was a deliberate cleanup, but what is left is close to a still image whenever the player stops to think.
- **Proposal.** After two seconds of rest, and every 4.6 s after, the cue ball lets go of two slow rings and a pair of glints run a lap of the rails from the bottom to the top, the way the Boot's light-cycles do. Dim, and it stops the instant a thumb goes down.
- **Why it fits.** Stays at rest brightness (bible law 3: the table rests at glow-0 and glow-1) and draws nothing on the felt where the player aims. The lap reuses the Boot transition's own image (§8.5).
- **Effort.** small (under a day)
- **Risk.** Low. You asked for nothing at rest once (the stick and lines). This is ambient rather than a control, but if it reads as clutter, reject it.
- **Where.** main.js frame loop; Polish._updateIdle
- **Prototype.** `window.__polish('idle', 'breathe')` (`lightline-polish-v1`), off by default.

### A5 · Full power: light from the ball, not a white screen

- **Problem.** A full-power strike fires two full-screen white flashes on the same frame (the strike's and the FULL POWER slam's), which stack to an almost completely white screen for a frame or two. On a phone at night that is harsh, and it hides the strike it is celebrating.
- **Proposal.** The strike lights from the cue ball outward (white core, orange falloff) and the screen-wide flash drops to under a third. Everything else about the full-power strike stays: the lines, the FULL POWER band, the jolt.
- **Why it fits.** Bible law 6: light comes from its cause. A flash from the ball says where the power went; a white screen says nothing.
- **Effort.** small (under a day)
- **Risk.** Med. This changes a review pick ("the strike's flash at full power", juice round 1). It keeps the flash and moves it onto the ball, but it is your call.
- **Where.** Juice.js strike (flash 0.55) and slam (flash 0.8); Polish.softFlash
- **Prototype.** `window.__polish('flash', 'soft')` (`lightline-polish-v1`), off by default.

## 5. UI and transitions

Menus, the score bar and the moments between screens.

### U1 · Menus: the home screen boots, panels arrive, buttons answer

- **Problem.** The home screen appears complete on the first frame, and moving between Home, Play, Settings and Achievements is a hard cut: one panel is hidden and the next shown. A tapped button shrinks by 2% and makes no sound. It is the one part of the game with no motion language.
- **Proposal.** On Home the title flickers on left to right, a line of light draws under it, and Play rises in. A panel's rows arrive one after another (the table list a row at a time). Every button dips and flashes under the thumb, with a tick.
- **Why it fits.** Uses the bible's own tokens: ease-boot (steps of 4) for the glyph flicker, ease-draw for lines drawing on. The result screen's buttons already "dip and flash the instant they are tapped" (§8.4); this makes every button do it.
- **Effort.** small (under a day)
- **Risk.** Low. A returning player sees the boot each time they open Home (0.9 s, never blocking a tap).
- **Where.** main.js showPanel; index.html .menu-btn:active; Polish.menuPanel, _bindPress
- **Prototype.** `window.__polish('menu', 'boot')` (`lightline-polish-v1`), off by default.

### U2 · Score bar: passing the target is a moment

- **Problem.** The target is what pays cash, and beating it mid-table is the first win of every table. Today the bar fills and a small tick appears next to the number. Nothing marks the moment, and it is easy to miss while the tally flies in.
- **Proposal.** When the score passes the target, a sweep of white crosses the bar, a ring leaves its end, and a TARGET BEATEN chip pops under it for a second, with a click and a rising fifth.
- **Why it fits.** Bible law 8: "a counter that jumps on its own is a bug", and any figure that changes is telegraphed loudly. The chip is a chamfered UI plate in the `you` cyan the result screen already uses for BEATEN.
- **Effort.** small (under a day)
- **Risk.** Low. It follows the tally landing by a quarter second, so two things happen close together at the top of the screen. Sequenced after the tally, not on top of it.
- **Where.** HUD.js update (target bar); Polish.targetBeaten
- **Prototype.** `window.__polish('target', 'beat')` (`lightline-polish-v1`), off by default.

## 6. Feel

Weight on contact, and how the game runs on a phone.

### F1 · Contact: a snap and a held beat when balls meet

- **Problem.** In a run, the cue ball hitting a ball has no weight. The hit-stop on contact was switched off when the score presenter took over the freeze, so unless that contact is about to sink a ball, the table does not pause at all: ten soft particles and the balls part.
- **Proposal.** A fan of sparks thrown across the line of the hit, a ring off the struck ball in its own color, and on a firm hit the table holds for 20 to 60 ms, longer the harder. The hold is time only: the physics takes the same steps and the aim line is untouched.
- **Why it fits.** Bible §6 lists "hit-stop 90" as a core duration and §8.1 freezes the table on the strike; this gives the second most common impact the same language at a third of the length.
- **Effort.** small (under a day)
- **Risk.** Med. A hit-stop on every firm contact changes rhythm on a busy break (overlapping stops take the longest, they do not add). The aim check (check-aim-run) must stay at 100%; it does not see hit-stops, but it is the gate.
- **Where.** main.js impact handler (hit-stop only when the score feed is off); Polish.contact
- **Prototype.** `window.__polish('contact', 'snap')` (`lightline-polish-v1`), off by default.

### F2 · Phones: measure frame time, then step quality down when it runs long

- **Problem.** On a 3x phone the game draws a 780×1386 half-float buffer through a five-level bloom every frame, even on a still table. Whether bloom runs is decided once, from the core count, which says little about the GPU: a budget phone with 8 slow cores gets the full pipeline, and nothing steps down if frames run long. I could not time a real phone GPU from here; these are counts and sizes.
- **Proposal.** First a frame-time probe (rolling average, shown with ?fps and reported in the anonymous stats). Then an adaptive step: above 22 ms for two seconds, pixel ratio 2 → 1.5; still above, bloom off for the session. Never stepping back up mid-table, so the look does not flicker.
- **Why it fits.** The look is unchanged on phones that keep up. On ones that do not, a steady frame rate is the bigger part of "feels polished".
- **Effort.** medium (1 to 2 days)
- **Risk.** Low. Without bloom the glow levels flatten (the bible's four levels lean on it). That is why it is the last step, not the first.
- **Where.** main.js buildComposer, bloomAllowed, resize; config.js RENDER

| --- | --- | --- |
| Drawing buffer, 390×844 @3x | 780×1386 | 585×1040 at ratio 1.5 |
| Pixels through bloom per frame | 1.08 M | 0.61 M, then 0 |
| Bloom decided by | core count ≥ 4, once | measured frame time |

## 7. Performance on a phone-sized screen

Measured in the built game at phone viewports. A software renderer cannot time a phone's GPU, so these are counts and sizes, not frame rates; the frame rate itself needs a device.

| | Today |
| --- | --- |
| Drawing buffer on a 390×844 @3x phone | 780×1386 (pixel ratio capped at 2, `RENDER.maxPixelRatio`) |
| Post-processing | Bloom (UnrealBloomPass, half-float target at full buffer size, 5 blur levels) whenever the device reports 4+ cores, then an output pass |
| GPU resources on Table 1 | 74 geometries, 18 textures, 21 shader programs |
| DOM at rest in play | about 390 nodes; a shot adds at most a few dozen short-lived ones and up to 6 running WAAPI animations |
| Quality fallback | None at run time: bloom is decided once, from the core count |

Findings:

- The scene itself is light. The cost on a phone is fill rate: a 1.08-megapixel half-float buffer through a five-level bloom every frame, at every moment, including at rest on a still table.
- The core-count test is a weak proxy. Budget Android phones report 8 cores with slow GPUs and get full bloom; nothing steps quality down if frames run long.
- The polish prototypes are DOM and WAAPI only (transform and opacity), a few short-lived nodes each, and add no draw calls. The rail and pocket effects use `mix-blend-mode: screen`, which is the one thing to watch on low-end GPUs.

Proposal (item F2): measure before changing. Add a frame-time probe (a rolling average of real frame time, visible with `?fps`), then an adaptive step: if the average stays above 22 ms for two seconds, drop the pixel ratio to 1.5, and if it still does, turn bloom off for the session; never step back up mid-table. Record the tier in the anonymous stats so the real distribution is known.
