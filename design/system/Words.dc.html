Table language · the words

Four nouns, five verbs, and no synonyms

A player learning a game is learning its vocabulary. Every second word for the same thing is a thing they have to learn twice — so there is exactly one word for each, and the card, the HUD and the text that flies off the table all use it.

Ballyou hit itNever "enemy", never "target". Called by its number when there is one that matters: the 8.
Pocketyou sink into itNever "goal", never "hole". Four kinds, and the kind is an adjective: gold pocket.
Padyou drive through itNever "ring", "zone", "pickup" or "injector". Also an adjective: freeze pad.
Wallyou bank off itRails, pillars and bumpers are all walls. The verb is bank, because that is what a billiards player calls it.

The verbs

hit — your ball touches a ball sink — a ball goes down a pocket bank — anything bounces off a wall drive through — anything crosses a pad scratch — your own ball goes down

Sink rather than pot: "pot" is the correct billiards term and the wrong game word, because half the audience has to be taught it first. Drive through rather than "run through" or "cross": it says the ball is being sent somewhere with force, which is the whole feeling of the shot.

The same word, in all three places

A new thing is named identically wherever it is mentioned, in the same session, within seconds of each other. Three sightings of one word teach it; one sighting each of three words teaches nothing.

How to write a card

One instruction, in the imperative, naming one thing. "Sink the 3." Not what it is for, not what it is worth, not what happens next.

The hint line carries the one fact that makes it possible, and only one. "Hitting a ball never breaks it. Only a pocket takes one off the table."

Highlight at most two words: the thing (red) and the action (green). The colours are the same on every card, so the colour is readable before the sentence is.

Never use a word the player has not met. If the card needs a new noun, that noun is what this board is for — and it is the only new one on it.
