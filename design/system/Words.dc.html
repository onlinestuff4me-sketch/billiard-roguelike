Table language · the words

Say it the way you would say it out loud

Every instruction uses a word a player already owns before they start. Hit, shoot, knock in, bounce — no billiards vocabulary, no invented nouns, and no second word for anything.

The verbs

shoot — what you do hit — your ball touches anything knock in — a ball goes down a pocket bounce — anything comes off a wall save — shots you did not spend

Knock in rather than sink or pot: both of those have to be taught before they can be used, and "knock it in" is what a person says while pointing at a table. Bounce rather than bank, for the same reason — bank is the correct billiards word and half the audience does not have it.

The one place jargon survives is foul, for knocking your own ball in, because there is no shorter way to say "that shot pays nothing" and every sport already uses it.

Every lesson card, and the one rule that is not a card

Knock the 3 in
Press anywhere and pull back, like a cue.
Knock the 2 in from an angle
Hit its far side. The white circle is where your ball ends up.
Four balls. Three shots.
One shot has to knock two in.
Go round the barrier
The 3 is blocked. Bounce off a side wall.
Take the green. Miss the red.
Green is always good. Red is always bad.
Don't knock your own ball in
That is a foul: the shot pays nothing and you start again from the spot.

How to write one

One instruction, imperative, naming one thing. Everything else goes on the second line, and the second line carries exactly one fact.

Two highlights at most: the thing (red) and the action (green) — the same two colours on every card, so the colour is readable before the sentence is. And they are the same two colours the felt uses, which is not a coincidence: red on a card and red on the table both mean "this one".

Never a word the player has not met. If a card needs a new noun, that card is the board that introduces it, and it is the only new noun on it.
