Table language · the architecture

Pockets are not on the table. They are cut into it.

A pocket should be the most obviously permanent thing on screen — a piece of the furniture that was there before the rack was set and will be there after it is cleared. So the frame swells outward around every pocket and the cushions break and flare into the mouth, exactly as they do on a real table. You are not looking at a lit target painted on the felt; you are looking at a hole with a railing round it.

Corner · the jaws

Both cushions stop short and their ends splay open, so the mouth is wider than the gap between the rails. The frame behind bulges into a full circle around the void.

Side · the break

The long cushion is interrupted rather than curved: two runs, two flared ends, and the frame swelling out behind. A break in a straight line reads from further away than a bend does.

The rules that come with it

All six are identicalThere are no pocket types any more. Every pocket pays the ball's number, at the multiplier you have built. Gold, upgrade and live pockets move onto the felt as pick-ups, where colour is allowed to mean something.
Colour never touches themA pocket is drawn in the table's own materials and nothing else. That is what stops it competing with the mint and red objects for the same glance — the eye can look for "a hole" without parsing hue at all.
The mouth is bigger than the holeThe flared jaws are wider than the capture radius, so a ball that looks like it is going in, goes in. The visual promise has to be more generous than the rule, never less.
Jaws do not rattleThe throat is drawn, not simulated. A ball is taken when its centre reaches the capture radius, exactly as before — no jaw bounces, no near-misses that the preview did not show. The prediction lines have to keep telling the truth.
Position is the only nameA contract that wants a specific pocket calls it by where it is — "the 8 in a corner" — because position is architecture too, and needs no colour to say it.

What this costs

Choosing which pocket used to be a real decision — gold for the multiplier, live for the gamble. That decision moves to the felt: which route you take across it, and which pick-ups and hazards that route crosses. It is the better place for it, because the felt is where the ball actually travels and the rails are only where it ends up. But it is a genuine loss of one axis, and worth watching in play.
