Table language · the allocation

Five channels, and nothing shares one

Colour was pulling in two directions at once: a cyan pocket and a cyan pick-up both shouting for the same attention while meaning completely different things. So the palette is now allocated. Each channel owns its hues outright, and no hue appears in two rows of this table.

ChannelHuesOwnsNever
YouThe cue ball, the aim line, the trajectory preview. The only cyan on the table is the shot you are about to take.Never on anything you are aiming at.
The tableThe frame, cushions, walls and pocket mouths. Architecture: always there, identical every room.Never on anything that can be collected or spent.
The rackThe balls. Amber solids 1–4, banded stripes 5–7, the black 8. Their colour says which ball, nothing more.Never red and never mint — a ball is neither good nor bad.
GoodEvery pick-up: points, multipliers, upgrades, freeze. If it is mint, hitting it helps you.Never on a pocket, a wall or a ball.
BadEvery hazard. If it is red, hitting it costs you something.Never on anything you are meant to aim at.

And within a channel, form says which

BallHas a number Raised, solid, numbered. Only a ball carries a number, and the number is what it pays.
PocketIs a hole in the table A void with the frame swelling round it. Six of them, always in the same six places.
Pick-upMint, dashed, open Flat on the felt. Hit it and something good happens; the glyph says what.
HazardRed, dashed, open The same flat form, the other meaning. One glance at the colour is the whole decision.

What this fixes

Four glowing rings, three meanings
→
Two pick-ups and two pockets, unmistakable

The two gold things and the two cyan things all became one of two answers: mint on the felt or a hole in the table. Gold and magenta leave the palette entirely — with pocket types gone there is nothing left for them to mean, and every hue that stays means exactly one thing.
