Table language · the one law

Four families. You can tell them apart with the colour turned off.

Right now a gold pad and a gold pocket are both glowing gold rings, because colour is carrying the whole job. It cannot. Form says what a thing is; colour says what it does to your score. Two channels, two jobs, and neither doing the other's work.

BallYou move it

Raised. Solid. Carries a number.

Only a ball has a number. The number is what it is worth, so anything numbered is something you are trying to move somewhere. Shape still says which archetype: cube, octagon, cylinder.

PocketA ball that gets here leaves

A hole. Dark centre, one solid unbroken rim.

Only a pocket has a dark centre. Absence reads faster than any colour: a hole in the felt looks like somewhere a ball goes and does not come back from, before you have read anything.

PadYou drive through it

Flat and open. Broken rim, nothing in the middle.

Only a pad has a dashed edge. A broken outline is a gap you can pass through, drawn as one. Nothing stops here and nothing is taken; something about the shot changes and the pad goes dark.

WallYou bounce off it

Raised, solid, unlit. Never numbered.

Walls are the only bright-free objects on the felt. If it glows, it does something. If it does not, it is geometry — and geometry is worth points, because every wall you take pays a rung on the multiplier.

What colour is for

Colour never says what a thing is — form already did that. Colour says one thing only: what it does to your score. Cyan pays points. Gold multiplies. Magenta upgrades. Orange-red costs you. So a gold pad and a gold pocket should share a hue — they both double — and the reader can still tell them apart instantly, because one is a hole and one is dashed.

This is also what makes the table readable without colour vision. Every discrimination above is a shape discrimination; hue is confirmation, never the carrier.
