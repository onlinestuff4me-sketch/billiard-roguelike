Table language · teaching it

One new word at a time, and never two in the same breath

Seven boards introduce everything: the cue, the pocket, the wall, and the two colours. After that each new object gets a room of its own to be met in, one banner, and nothing else new on the table.

One new word per boardA board that introduces two nouns teaches neither. If a card needs a word the player has not met, that word is what the board exists for.
The board holds only its subjectEmpty felt around it. Clutter reads as difficulty, and a lesson has to be able to promise that the table contains its subject and nothing else.
Teach green and red togetherBoards 05 and 06 run back to back on purpose. The two colours are one idea and only mean anything as a pair.
Teach a difference by showing bothBoard 05 puts a green pick-up and a pocket on one table, because you cannot learn that they are different things from a board with only one of them on it.
Name it as it happensThe text that flies off the table uses the word from the card, within the same second. Feedback is the second sighting, and the second sighting is what makes it stick.
Label the first sightingThe first time an object appears in a run, a chip sits beside it for three seconds. Once per type per run, then the table speaks for itself.

The first-sighting chip

Anchored beside the object in world space, three seconds, once per type per run. It names the thing and what it does in the words the lesson used — so a player who skipped the tutorial still meets every word once, in context, the moment it first matters.
