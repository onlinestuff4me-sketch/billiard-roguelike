Table language · teaching it

Five boards. The first one is already the whole game.

No board teaches a control in isolation. Board one is a real shot into a real pocket — the gesture and the rule arrive together, because a player who has only been told to "hit a ball" has not yet been told what the game is. Everything after that adds exactly one idea, and two of the five teach by constraint rather than by card.

01the cut
Knock the 3 in, on an angleThe white circle is where your ball ends up — keep it off the pocket.
This was three boards: "knock it in", "find the angle" and "watch where you end up" all ran the same table with the same ball. Three identical tables in a row do not read as three lessons, they read as the game being stuck. Measured window: 6.5°, from a rest 7° off it.
02the combination
Use the 4 as your cueThe 4 and the 1 line up on the lit pocket.
The first board where the object ball does the work. The two balls sit a proper distance apart now, so the hand-off is visibly a hand-off rather than a nudge. The far ball drops across 4.5°.
03the angled combination
Now on an angleHit the 6 on the side that sends it across into the 2.
Judged on the hand-off, not the pot: an angled combination pot measures at about 1.5°, which is tournament accuracy on a third board. The two balls were then pulled five units apart so the route is visible before it is played — which widened the window rather than narrowing it. 3.5°, from a rest 2° off it.
04the bank
Go round, off the cushionYour cue starts pointed at the near cushion.
The cue now rests aimed at the wall it must use, so the route is visible before the first attempt, and the 3 sits up off the side pocket with the corner called. Judged on bank-and-strike: a banked pot is a 1° shot. Window: 2.5° from a rest 2° off it.
05the budget
Four balls. Three shots.Both lit pockets are yours.
four balls
Two pockets stay lit for the whole board, because the plan spans the strokes. The single stroke that drops one in the side AND one in the far corner was searched for and does not exist — after the first cut the cue has lost most of its speed and its departure is nearly fixed, so reaching a second ball twelve units away is a coincidence, not a plan.
06green & red
The red is in the wayThe lazy line runs over a mine. Go just above it, through the green.
The choice, in one table: the line you would take without looking is poisoned, and the line that pays is barely off it. The green was meant to sit behind a bank — measured, a route that banks, collects a pick-up and then pots is worth about a degree, so it is a thread instead. 4.5° wide, and it still costs you the easy line.
One new idea per boardA board that introduces two teaches neither. If a card needs a word the player has not met, that word is what the board exists for.
Teach the budget by making it biteBoard 03 has no explanation on it. Four balls and three shots is the lesson, and the player works out what that means faster than any card could tell them.
Teach a pair as a pairGreen and red arrive on the same table, on the two routes the player is choosing between. Either one alone is just a coloured circle.
The board holds only its subjectEmpty felt around it. A lesson has to be able to promise that the table contains the thing it is about and nothing else.
Unfailable, but not freeRunning out of shots on board 03 re-racks it with a nudge — no hull, no score, no run lost. The budget is real; the consequence is not, yet.
Name it as it happensThe text that flies off the table uses the word from the card, in the same second. The second sighting is what makes it stick.

What the tutorial no longer covers, and where it went

The 8 goes last was a board and is now a room banner — it is a rule about balls the player already knows, so it does not need a table of its own. Freeze was never a board and stays a room arrival, because it cannot be taught until there is a shot worth interrupting. Everything else that used to have a board has been folded into the five above.

The first-sighting chip

Anchored beside the object in world space, three seconds, once per type per run. It names the thing and what it does in the words the lesson used — so a player who skipped the tutorial still meets every word once, in context, the moment it first matters.
