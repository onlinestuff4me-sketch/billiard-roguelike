The economy

How you win, and how you score

One rule change carries the redesign: a ball is never destroyed by being hit — only by being driven into a target. The cue ball stops being a weapon and becomes a delivery system, which is what makes the shot, rather than the power, the thing you are choosing.

01

Pots

Every ball is numbered. It pays its number × 100, at the multiplier standing the moment it drops.

02

Strokes saved

Every stroke left in the budget at room end pays 500 × room number. The skill income.

03

Multipliers picked up

Gold rings on the felt and gold pockets on the rail double whatever you have built this stroke.

04

Multipliers earned

Banks, extra balls touched, extra balls dropped. Tricks compound inside a single stroke.

The multiplier ladder

Each stroke opens at ×1 and climbs while the table is still moving:

+1each rail the cue ball banks off
+1each ball the cue ball touches
+1each ball sunk, or driven through a gate
×2a gold ring or gold pocket doubles what is built

Points are paid at the instant a ball drops, at the multiplier standing then — so the order of a route is worth real money. Bank before you pot; take the gold ring late, not early.

This is the escalating pentatonic run you already hear. The notes climb with the multiplier, so the audio is the scoreboard.

The stroke ramp

The rack grows and the budget shrinks. Spare is the number of strokes you can afford to waste — once it goes negative, a single stroke has to do double duty.

Because saved strokes pay 500 × room, the ramp cuts both ways: the rooms where a spare stroke is hardest to keep are the rooms where keeping one is worth the most.

Failing a contract is not instant death. The rack breaks loose and every ball still standing takes a bite out of your hull — so a bad room costs you the next few rooms.

Where power-ups fit. Destroying a ball with a direct hit is no longer a rule — it is a prize. A boon that lets a full-power strike shatter on contact is genuinely build-changing precisely because the base game forbids it. Same shape for the others: freeze (stop the table mid-stroke), +1 stroke per room, rails count double, the multiplier survives into the next stroke. Each one bends a rule the player has already learned.
