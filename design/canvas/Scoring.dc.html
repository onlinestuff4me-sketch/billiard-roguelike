How you win, and how you score

One rule change carries the whole redesign: a ball is never destroyed by being hit. It is destroyed by being driven into a target. The cue ball stops being a weapon and becomes a delivery system — which is what makes the shot, not the power, the thing the player is choosing.

How you win

The room ends when its goal is met — the contract is filled, par is beaten, the threshold is reached. Then the two doors open exactly as they do now, and you shoot into the one you want.

The run ends when your hull hits zero. Balls hurt you by touching you, by being fired back out of a live pocket, and by whatever the room's own pressure is. The number you are playing for is your run score.

Every room states its goal in one sentence at the top of the screen, and shows progress against it. Nothing about winning is inferred.

How you score

Every ball is numbered. A ball pays its number × 100, at the multiplier standing the moment it goes down.

The stroke opens at ×1 and climbs while the table is still moving. Order matters — bank first, pot last:

+1each rail the cue ball banks off
+1each ball the cue ball touches
+1each ball driven into a target
×2a gold target doubles what has been built

This is the escalating pentatonic run you already hear — the notes climb with the multiplier, so the audio is the scoreboard.

Score

Takes the ball, pays its number. The bread-and-butter target.

Gold

Doubles the multiplier you have built, then pays at the doubled rate.

Upgrade

Pays nothing. Adds a boon pick to the room's reward instead.

Live

Pays double, then fires the ball back onto the table at speed, at you.

Scratch

The cue ball into any target. The stroke's score is voided and you re-spot.

Where power-ups fit. Destroying a ball with a direct hit is not a rule any more — it is a boon. An earned upgrade that lets a full-power strike shatter on contact is a genuine build-changing pick precisely because the base game says it cannot happen. Same for a boon that turns every rail into a scoring target, or one that keeps the multiplier alive across two strokes.
